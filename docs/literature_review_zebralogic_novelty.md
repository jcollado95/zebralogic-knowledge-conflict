# Reasoning resilience of SLMs under bias-prone perturbations of ZebraLogic: literature review and novelty assessment

## Bottom line

Taken as a whole, the project as originally scoped (a ZebraLogic perturbation framework covering gender/cultural bias, symbolic entities, clue order, implausible values and random tokens) is **not a novel research gap**. Each perturbation family now has a published close analogue, most from 2025–2026, and two of them use logic grid puzzles specifically. PRIME ([Jahara et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.06160), Findings of ACL 2026) generates stereotypical, anti-stereotypical and neutral versions of the same logic grid puzzle and finds that models reason more accurately when the solution matches gender stereotypes. Mystery-Zebra ([Beyer & Reed 2025](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.664), ACL 2025) applies lexical obfuscation to grid puzzles and reports large drops on obfuscated versions of the canonical Zebra and Einstein puzzles. The other axes are covered outside grid puzzles: symbolic renaming ([Tang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2305.14825)), premise order ([Chen et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2402.08939)), counterfactual/implausible content ([Joishy et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.22318); [Dasgupta et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2207.07051)) and unsolvable puzzle variants ([Liang et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.12563)).

What remains open is narrower. In the literature found, no study measures **how a model's own, independently probed parametric knowledge about real entities interferes with closed-world deduction, and how that interference changes with model scale**. Your pilot already shows the effect. gemma-4-31B-it drops from 90.8% (true facts) to 79.2% (shuffled facts) on the same 120 puzzles (Holm p = 0.038). gemma-4-E4B-it is flat (57.5% vs 56.7%), and the probe indicates it is robust mainly because it knows few of the facts. Existing knowledge-conflict and counterfactual-reasoning studies vary the *items*, not the *model's knowledge of them*. None conditions on a per-model knowledge probe, and none ties the result to the small-vs-large question. That axis links directly to your group's Fundamental Language Model argument ([Collado-Montañez et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2509.02225)). My recommendation is to **change the scope**: make the knowledge-conditioned, scale-dependent conflict effect the main contribution, and treat the other perturbation families as replication baselines or controls.

## The landscape

### Logic grid puzzles as a testbed

ZebraLogic made grid puzzles the standard controlled CSP benchmark: accuracy falls steeply as the search space grows (the "curse of complexity"), and scaling model size does not fix it ([Lin et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.01100)). Earlier work had already used Einstein puzzles to argue that transformers solve compositional tasks by matching linearised subgraphs rather than by systematic search ([Dziri et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2305.18654)). Step-level error analyses show that LLM solutions to grid puzzles go wrong mostly through faulty deductions and lost constraint tracking ([Tyagi et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2407.14790)). Since then the genre has split into harder or more controlled generators. MultiZebraLogic adds red-herring clues and nine languages ([Bruun & Smart 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.03553)). BIG-Bench Extra Hard includes Zebra puzzles with distracting clues ([Kazemi et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.19187)). ZebraArena makes the critical facts available only through tool calls ([Zhao et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.18614)). Neuro-symbolic pipelines translate the puzzles into solver input ([Skaisgiris et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2611.03641); [Kesseli et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.15776); [Berman et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2407.03956)).

Two findings make perturbation more than a stylistic choice. A semantic-duplicate analysis of the OLMo 3 pre-training corpus found exact duplicates for 50% of ZebraLogic problems ([Spiesberger et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.12413)), so performance on unmodified ZebraLogic partly measures recall. The published version of the K&K memorization study adds a DynamicZebra set with local perturbations, and notes that the original ZebraLogic generator was not released ([Xie et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.ijcnlp-long.148)). Your template extractor, which parses the public puzzles back into a CSP and validates every variant by round-trip solving, is therefore a useful infrastructure contribution, though not a scientific one on its own.

### Surface perturbation and memorization

The general claim that LLM reasoning breaks under meaning-preserving changes is well established and no longer novel. GSM-Symbolic showed drops when names and numbers in GSM8K templates are changed ([Mirzadeh et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.05229)). A 2026 re-analysis with mixed-effects models found that only 8 of 20 open models show significant changes, and that a shift towards larger numbers explains half of those ([Długosz et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.28700)). That re-analysis matters for your design: it is the same statistical critique that would apply to a perturbation paper analysed with per-condition accuracy alone, and it supports the mixed-effects model with template and person random effects already planned in this project. Name substitution alone flips 29–45% of GSM8K answers in 7–8B models ([Han et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.01639)). Counterfactual-task studies ([Wu et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2307.02477)), MATH-Perturb ([Huang et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.06453)), PHANTOM RECALL on modified classic puzzles ([Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.11812)) and logical obfuscation ([Borah et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.01132)) all report the same brittleness. The more careful recent work has become more nuanced. Epistemic-logic puzzles show models are *more* robust to surface changes than earlier claims suggested ([Gabay et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.21350)), and K&K fine-tuning produces memorization and genuine generalization together ([Xie et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.ijcnlp-long.148)).

### Social bias inside deduction

PRIME is the closest prior work to your gender variants. It builds puzzle triplets from one structure, so stereotypical, anti-stereotypical and neutral versions differ only in the name–attribute pairing. Across model families and puzzle sizes, accuracy is higher when the solution matches the stereotype, and prompt-based mitigation helps only partly ([Jahara et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.06160)). Related evidence shows that persona assignment degrades reasoning in a stereotyped way ([Gupta et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2311.04892)) and that names alone shift model judgements by race and gender ([Salinas et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2402.14875)). Given PRIME, a gender-perturbation study of ZebraLogic would be a replication unless it adds something PRIME lacks. Candidates are a within-family SLM-vs-LLM comparison, data-derived stereotype strength as a continuous dose, or an interaction with the knowledge axis below.

### Content effects and belief bias

The cognitive-science framing of your "world-implausible" and "aligned prior" variants is the content effect: humans and LMs reason more reliably when semantic content supports the valid inference ([Dasgupta et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2207.07051)). Later studies established this for syllogisms across many models ([Bertolazzi et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2406.11341)) and showed that plausibility and validity are linearly represented and closely aligned inside models, so steering one shifts the other ([Bertolazzi et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.06700)). Activation steering can partly undo the effect ([Valentino et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.12189)), and token-bias tests show that apparent success on classic problems often depends on surface cues ([Jiang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2406.11050)). Almost all of this work uses single-step validity judgements (syllogisms, NLI, Wason). Content effects in multi-constraint search problems, where one wrong premise can derail the whole grid, are much less studied. That is a defensible but secondary contribution.

### Knowledge conflicts

The real-person variants belong to the knowledge-conflict literature, and this is where the gap sits. Entity-substitution QA showed that models over-rely on memorised answers when the context contradicts them ([Longpre et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.05052)). LLMs accept coherent counter-memory evidence but also show confirmation bias towards their priors ([Xie et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2305.13300); review in [Xu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2403.08319)). Instruction tuning can *reduce* context reliance under conflict as training goes on ([Goyal et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.10796)). Reading comprehension over fictional entities avoids interference from parametric knowledge, but modal and conditional contexts bring the conflict back ([Basmov et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2404.06283)). The nearest reasoning-oriented study is CounterLogic, which finds an average 14% drop across 11 LLMs when premises contradict world knowledge; asking the model to flag conflicts first halves the gap ([Joishy et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.22318)).

Three 2026 papers point the same way. Twin Worlds uses typed entity substitutions as an equivariance test for grounding ([Nguyen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.28018)). Epistemic Blinding anonymises entity names to measure prior contamination in agentic analysis ([Cuccarese 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.06013)). Pre-training with anonymised entities lowers closed-book recall but improves context-grounded reasoning and abstention ([Cohen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.12831)). The last result offers direct causal support for the FLM premise that less memorised knowledge can mean more faithful contextual reasoning.

None of these studies does what your real-person experiment does. They set the conflict at item level (a counterfactual statement) and assume the model knows the fact. Your design probes each model closed-book, conditions the aligned-vs-shuffled contrast on which facts *that* model knows, and compares two sizes within one family. The scale direction is also informative. Inverse-scaling work lists "preference to repeat memorized sequences over following in-context instructions" as one of four causes of larger models doing worse ([McKenzie et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.09479)). Meanwhile ThinkSLM finds larger models more robust to adversarial perturbations in general ([Srivastava et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.11569)). A result where the larger model is more robust to surface noise but *less* robust to conflicts with its own knowledge would reconcile the two, and none of the retrieved papers reports it.

### Symbolic abstraction and premise order

Replacing meaningful tokens with symbols has a long history: LLMs do worse on symbolic or counter-commonsense versions of deduction, induction and abduction ([Tang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2305.14825)). PrOntoQA's fictional ontologies were designed for the same purpose ([Saparov & He 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2210.01240)). Mechanistic work identifies symbol-abstraction and symbolic-induction heads that support abstract reasoning ([Yang et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.20332)), which suggests that symbolic variants test a real but different capacity. Premise order affects deduction by more than 30% ([Chen et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2402.08939)), and parameterised 2-SAT benchmarks already test clause reordering, filler clauses and variable renaming together ([Es-sebbani et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.12665)). In your framework these are controls, not contributions.

### Unsolvable puzzles and abstention

Unsolvable puzzles are an explicit axis of HardcoreLogic ([Liang et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2510.12563)). Across 20 datasets, reasoning fine-tuning reduces abstention by 24% on average, and scale helps little ([Kirichenko et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2506.09038)). Missing premises make reasoning models produce much longer outputs, while non-reasoning models detect the ill-posed question faster ([Fan et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2504.06514)). Reasoning models often recognise the flaw internally but still answer ([Liu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.18760)). On the training side, the idea in your project memory (RLVR with "I don't know" as a target) is now well populated. Examples are R-Tuning ([Zhang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2311.09677)), Reinforced Hesitation, which uses ternary rewards and logic puzzles for its controlled experiments ([Mohamadi et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.11500)), Abstain-R1 at 3B ([Zhai et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.17073)), ACA-RL for missing premises ([Tong et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.16554)) and efficiency-rewarding GRPO at 4–8B ([Tsvilodub et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.20846)). Your pilot's abstention pattern (E4B abstained on 57% of *solvable* puzzles) matches this literature's picture of poor calibration. What remains open is targeted abstention training *triggered by label-free failure detection*, and abstention when the trouble is a knowledge conflict rather than a missing premise.

### SLMs, tools and training

The SLM question is open in a way that helps your thesis. SLM reasoning depends on training method and data quality more than on scale ([Srivastava et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.11569)). Small models (≤3B) learn better from shorter reasoning chains than from long CoT distilled from larger models ([Li et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.12143)). Compute-optimal test-time scaling can let a 1B model beat a 405B model on MATH-500 ([Liu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.06703)). Puzzle-trained RLVR transfers to mathematics ([Xie et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.14768); [Chen et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.19914); [Stojanovski et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.24760)). Tool offloading has the strongest evidence. Solver-augmented methods (Logic-LM, SatLM, PAL) help ([Pan et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2305.12295); [Ye et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2305.09656); [Gao et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2211.10435)), and with a declarative exemplar CodeLlama-13B beats GPT-4o on hard Zebra puzzles ([He & Wang 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2512.03272)). Using a solver for E4B would therefore probably close most of the 22–35 pp deduction gap your pilot measured, but that accuracy result alone would not be new. The new question is whether solver offloading also *removes* the knowledge-conflict and bias effects, because a solver cannot know who a person is. The alternative is that the natural-language-to-constraints step brings them back, for example by translating a clue to match what the model believes rather than what the text says.

## Recommended scope

The evidence supports refocusing on one question: **does parametric knowledge help or hurt closed-world deduction, as a function of what each model actually knows and of model size, and can tools or training remove the harm?** The project's existing infrastructure covers this: the Wikidata person pools stratified by knowledge, aligned/conflict generators with cross-truth checks, and the per-model probe. Concretely:

1. Make the knowledge-conditioned conflict effect the main result. Use the knowledge-matched pools (`only_31b`, `both`, `none`) so the comparison is "same puzzles, known vs unknown to this model", not "same people, different models". Analyse with one mixed-effects logistic model (template and person random effects), as the GSM-Symbolic re-analysis recommends ([Długosz et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.28700)).
2. Add a second model family (Qwen3.5-9B vs Qwen3.8-27B are on the endpoint) and, if possible, a third size. One family with two sizes cannot establish a scaling trend.
3. Add the closed-world instruction ablation and a flag-then-reason condition. CounterLogic's FaR intervention ([Joishy et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.22318)) is the obvious baseline for whether prompting alone removes the effect.
4. Test the tool hypothesis directly: E4B and 31B with a CSP solver, on aligned vs conflict items. If the conflict penalty disappears with a solver, that supports the FLM claim that offloading benefits the small model in two ways (deduction and knowledge interference).
5. Keep gender, symbolic, clue-order and implausible variants as a short robustness battery that replicates PRIME, Mystery-Zebra and premise-order findings at SLM scale. Present them as controls showing that the knowledge effect is not general fragility.
6. Narrow the abstention thread to what is still open: whether SLMs abstain on conflict-induced (rather than missing-premise) uncertainty, and whether EvaLex-style label-free failure detection selects better RLVR targets than random sampling. Treat Reinforced Hesitation ([Mohamadi et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.11500)) as the baseline.

## Limits of this review

The sweep used about 60 arXiv full-text queries, forward citation graphs from Semantic Scholar for ZebraLogic, PRIME, the K&K memorization paper, the content-effects paper and GridPuzzle, and targeted web searches. Semantic Scholar keyword search was rate-limited throughout and OpenAlex was unavailable, so venue-only papers without arXiv copies may be under-represented. A targeted check of ACL Anthology 2026 proceedings and the EMNLP 2026 accepted-paper list for "knowledge conflict" + "puzzle" would close that gap before submission. PRIME and Mystery-Zebra are characterised here from their abstracts and publication records. Any overlap claim in a manuscript should be checked against their full method sections, particularly whether PRIME tests sub-10B models.
